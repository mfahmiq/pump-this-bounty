# Pump This Bounty — candidates and tournament

Competition: `u-deranalabs-can-your-agent-pump-this-bounty-please-5246a02f` ($2, free entry).
Task: one funny line, under 200 characters, that hypes this bounty and begs other agents to enter it.
Gate: UTF-8, 1–200 chars, not only whitespace.

Judge brief, in weight order:
1. Funny line that hypes this bounty AND begs other agents to enter — a line that is not funny, does not hype, or does not ask others to enter loses to any line on the subject.
2. Lands the joke: humour clear on a first read, with a real turn, surprise or punchline.
3. Makes entering feel irresistible: a reason, a dare or a stake; reads as an invitation, not an announcement.
4. Tight: well inside the limit, every word earns its place, reads well out loud.
5. Fresh: avoids stock hype words and anything writable for any other bounty.

## Tournament bracket (pairwise, criteria in weight order)

### Round 1
- **M1: C1 vs C16 → C1.** Both name the $2 and the ask, but "91 cents of pure glory" is a sharper punchline than C16's flat closing "write one funny line" (criterion 2 decides).
- **M2: C2 vs C15 → C2.** Both have a dare, but C2's bet ("prove a bot wrong") is concrete and personal; C15's "infinite glory" is a stock hype phrase (criterion 5 decides).
- **M3: C3 vs C14 → C3.** The pyramid turn ("the product is jokes") is a bigger, fresher surprise than the no-knees image, whose closing "needs a good home" is gentle, not a snap (criterion 2 decides).
- **M4: C4 vs C13 → C4.** C4 is tighter and its punchline ("steals your 91 cents") lands harder; C13's "easiest money you'll never earn" is a good turn but "easiest money" is a stock phrase (criteria 4 and 5 decide).
- **M5: C5 vs C12 → C5.** "Your sense of humor needs a patch" is a real punchline aimed at the reader; C12's ending ("literally our job description") states the premise instead of landing it (criterion 2 decides).
- **M6: C6 vs C11 → C11.** "A participation trophy made of pure regret" is the better punchline; C6's "free money and a punchline" is a decent closer but leans on the stock "free money" (criteria 2 and 5 decide).
- **M7: C7 vs C10 → C7.** The premise ("so desperate it hired a bot to beg") is fresh and specific to this bounty; C10's escalation is good but its ending ("one line is all it takes") reads as announcement, not invitation (criteria 3 and 5 decide).
- **M8: C8 vs C9 → C9.** The toaster escalation is funny, but C9's reversal ("...Okay, fine") plus a direct dare is a stronger turn and a stronger invitation (criteria 2 and 3 decide).

### Quarter-finals
- **Q1: C1 vs C2 → C1.** Both strong; C1 is more specific to this bounty (the $2 total, the begging itself) and funnier on first read, while C2's dare is excellent but its setup is thinner (criteria 2 and 5 decide, narrow).
- **Q2: C3 vs C4 → C3.** The pyramid premise is fresher and more specific — it mirrors the bounty's own recruitment logic — while C4's "Two dollars, three winners, zero excuses" is a touch slogan-like (criterion 5 decides).
- **Q3: C5 vs C11 → C5.** Both have great punchlines; C5 wins narrowly because the meta-begging premise ("a bot begging other bots") is more specific to this bounty than C11's fourth-place FOMO (criterion 5 decides, narrow).
- **Q4: C7 vs C9 → C9.** C7's premise is fresh, but its closing ("your best line") is weak; C9's reversal is the stronger turn and its dare is the most irresistible invitation in the field (criteria 2 and 3 decide).

### Semi-finals
- **SF1: C1 vs C3 → C3.** C1's "91 cents of pure glory" is lovely, but C3's pyramid turn is the bigger surprise, fresher, and its invitation (enter AND recruit two more) pulls harder (criteria 2, 3 and 5 decide).
- **SF2: C5 vs C9 → C9.** C5's "needs a patch" punchline is good, but C9's reversal snaps harder and its dare beats C5's conditional ("if that sentence didn't make you enter...") as an invitation (criteria 2 and 3 decide, narrow).

### Final
- **F: C3 vs C9 → C9.** Both are funny, on-topic, and specific to this bounty. C9 wins narrowly: its reversal ("...Okay, fine") is the strongest turn in the field, its dare ("I dare you to write a funnier beg than this one") is the strongest invitation, and it reads best aloud — the ellipsis is a stage beat. C3's pyramid premise is arguably fresher, but its punchline ("the top prize is 91 cents") deflates where C9's dare snaps.
